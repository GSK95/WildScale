Six things WildScale does now

It sees the shot when you stop

Rest the playhead on a frame and WildScale shows what is in it: people, faces, cars, phones, titles. Click one to make it a Must show or Must hide mark, or leave them and let WildScale decide.

Turn auto detect off whenever you like. Shift + C still captures the frame and finds what is in it.

Cuts inside dissolves and fades

Scene Edit Detection now recognizes soft transitions. A dissolve is cut where the new shot takes over, and a fade through black is cut in the black, so the shot after it can be rescaled on its own.

A slow zoom or a lone fade from black is not a cut, so it stays whole.

Stop means stop, and it cleans up

Press Stop in the panel and the run halts at once. Everything the run already changed is put back: Scale and Position, crops, blur layers and the cuts it made.

Your timeline is never left half done, so you can stop whenever something looks wrong.

Every cut piece stays easy to touch

After a cut, each piece is linked to its own sound and selects like any clip. Select at playhead, then change Scale and Position in Effect Controls or drag in the Program Monitor, with your usual shortcuts.

This holds for WildScale's own cuts and for Premiere's Scene Edit Detection.

Uses both graphics cards

On a laptop with NVIDIA and AMD or Intel graphics, WildScale puts both to work. A long clip is split between the two cards and the processor, so finding cuts takes about half the time, and each frame of analysis goes to whichever card is free first.

Prefer one card? Settings has Fastest card only.

Learns from the way you edit

Every reference you set and every clip you move by hand becomes a measured decision in your own Your edits database. After three choices for one kind of shot, your habit outranks the built-in styles, and Like my own edits frames the whole timeline your way.

It learns by itself after each run, on the processor, so Premiere keeps its graphics card. It saves numbers, never frames.

How it works

Knows your speaker

Set the framing once. Matching shots follow it, and your own habits teach it more with every run.

Keeps titles whole

Text that leaves the crop gets a small move or a clean back blur.

Stays on your computer

Frames are analyzed locally. Your footage is never uploaded.

Finds every cut

Reads each frame on both graphics cards, cuts dissolves and fades, and keeps every piece linked to its sound.

Pastes your framing

Matching shots get your exact Scale and Position, or you copy them by hand.

Works within your memory

Settings checks your system and adjusts to the memory Premiere leaves free.
