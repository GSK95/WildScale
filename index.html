For Adobe Premiere Pro on Windows

Frame one shot.
WildScale reframes the rest.

Reframing a long edit for Reels usually means going clip by clip, moving the same speaker into the same spot. WildScale does that pass for you, live on your timeline.

Built the way editors work

Follows your framing

Set your main speaker once. WildScale recognizes the same camera setup across your cuts and applies your Position and Scale.

Keeps titles whole

When a title leaves the crop, it tries a small move first. If that is not enough, it shows the whole frame for as long as the title is on screen.

Clean back blur

Two layers and no more: an ambient blurred layer below, and your shot fitted to the frame above with a soft drop shadow.

Notices motion

Animated transitions, split screens and graphics flying in are split into takes, so each one gets its own framing.

Groups your shots

Clips are sorted into speaker, titles, graphics, repeated shots and b-roll. One click selects a group, ready for Paste Attributes.

Learns your fixes

Mark what must show or stay hidden, and fix a clip by hand. WildScale remembers your choices for future projects.

How it works

  1. Frame a shot. Set one clip of your speaker the way you like it.
  2. Remember it. Select the clip and add it as a reference.
  3. Click Rescale. Watch each clip change in your open sequence. Stop whenever you like and keep what is done.

Private by design

Frames are analyzed on your own computer. Your footage is never uploaded.

Requirements

  • Adobe Premiere Pro 26.5.2 or later
  • Windows 10 or 11, 64-bit
  • WildScale Setup, a free companion installer
  • FFmpeg, only for motion detection
